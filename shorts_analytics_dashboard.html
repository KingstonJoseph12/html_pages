Step 5

RANKED DIAGNOSIS

Three causes. Ranked by evidence weight. Plus what the data can't tell you, and the one finding that would have been invisible without this analysis.

1
Primary cause
Automation-pattern saturation and homogenization
Weekly uploads: 4 → 42 at go-live. Title length IQR collapsed to 47–52 chars. Hashtag count locked to exactly 3. Duplicate title stems: median 1,043 views vs 1,168 for unique stems. The algorithm may be classifying these as repetitive content.
2
Secondary cause
Retention weakness from clip length drift
Median avg % viewed fell 94.48 → 89.72 (−4.76 pts). Rendered duration vs retention: r=−0.608. POST 15–19s clips average 92.49% retention vs 109.84% for 10–14s clips. Retention is the primary Shorts ranking signal.
3
Tertiary cause
Subscriber conversion degradation
Aggregate subs/view: 0.684% → 0.499% (−27%). Mean subs/video: 9.98 → 7.29. Likes held steady (1.58% → 1.63%), so this isn't general disengagement — automation may be attracting a less sub-prone audience profile.
⚡ Surprise finding

The view numbers look fine. That's the trap.

PRE median views: 1,073 POST median views: 1,071 — nearly identical. Without this analysis, you'd look at views and think automation is working. The decay is in CTR (−24.6%), retention (−4.76 pts median), and subscriber conversion (−27%) — the precursors that predict future view collapse if left unaddressed.

What the data cannot tell us
Shorts shelf traffic source breakdown — whether views come from Shorts shelf vs search vs suggested surfaces
Swipe-away rate and exact retention curve shape (not just average percentage)
First-hour and first-24-hour velocity — critical for diagnosing fast-die patterns
Rewatch rate — high rewatch signals are a major Shorts distribution trigger
Whether YouTube is explicitly classifying repeated title/format patterns as repetitive content
Step 6

ACTION PLAN

Five ranked recommendations. Every one cites a specific number. Every one has a falsifiable 2-week success metric.

1
Upload automation config
Cut cadence from 6/day to 3/day for two weeks
Evidence (H2): Weekly uploads jumped 4→42. Within POST, the highest-volume week (48 uploads) had the lowest median views (954) among 3 POST weeks. r=−0.9998 between weekly count and mean views. Keep only the three strongest daily slots: 17:00, 19:00, and 21:00/23:00 IST.
If this works: POST median views should rise from 1,071 to at least 1,300 within 2 weeks. Aggregate subs/view should rise from 0.499% to at least 0.60%.
2
Clip editing
Cap rendered clip duration at 14 seconds
Evidence (H5 + 4c): Rendered duration vs retention: r=−0.608. POST 10–14s clips averaged 109.84% retention vs 92.49% for 15–19s. POST top 5 all cluster at 8–13s. POST bottom 5 all cluster at exactly 16s. This is the most consistent signal in the entire dataset.
If this works: median avg % viewed should rise from 89.72% to at least 100% within 2 weeks.
3
Title / Metadata
Break the template — prevent title stem reuse, vary hashtag count
Evidence (H2 + 4d): POST title IQR collapsed to 47–52 chars and hashtag count locked to exactly 3. Duplicate title stems had median 1,043 views vs 1,168 for unique stems. Videos with 5–6 hashtags had mean views of 2,842 vs 1,266 for exactly 3. Reject hashtag-only titles (6 videos, median 889 views).
If this works: mean CTR should recover from 1.46% to at least 1.80% within 2 weeks.
4
Audio pool
Weight by goal: double-weight top tracks, pause bottom three
Evidence (H7 + 4f): "Small Creator Struggles" leads reach at 5,275 mean views. "Have a great day" (merged) leads subscriber conversion at ~0.94% subs/view. Weakest tracks ("We can play together" 934, "Hope you have a great day" 960, "He wasn't ready" 989) are dragging the average. Also merge the duplicate "have a great day" / "Have a great day" labels.
If this works: known-audio videos should average at least 2,000 views and 0.70% subs/view within 2 weeks.
5
Scheduling
Drop 1:00 and 3:00 IST slots first when reducing cadence
Evidence (H1 + 4e): The 0–5 IST hour bucket had median 913 views vs 1,072 for 18–23 IST and 1,235 for 12–17 IST. Automation currently targets 1:00 and 3:00 IST heavily (8–9 jobs each). Friday was the worst-performing weekday (median 884 views). Move freed slots to Thursday (best median: 1,045) and Saturday (1,146).
If this works: evening-slot median views should stay at or above 1,200, and overall POST median should beat 1,071 within 2 weeks.
